The 'Stay Safe Online' Checklist for College Students

1. The Golden Rule of UPI: Drill this into your head—you ONLY enter your UPI PIN to send money or check your balance. If an app asks for your PIN to receive money, it’s 100% a scam. Cancel the transaction immediately.

2. Double-check the receiver's name: Before you hit send at the campus canteen or when splitting a bill with someone you just met, actually look at the name that pops up on your UPI app. Make sure it matches who you're supposed to be paying.

3. Stop flexing your barcodes: Posting aesthetic Insta stories of your concert tickets, flight boarding passes, or even your new college ID is a terrible idea. Scammers can pull a ton of personal info just by scanning those visible barcodes.

4. Ditch the recycled passwords: Using "Rahul@2004" for your student portal, Netflix, and your banking app is a disaster waiting to happen. Use a password manager so you can use random, strong passwords without having to memorize them.

5. Turn on 2FA for everything: Two-Factor Authentication is a lifesaver. Turn it on for WhatsApp, Instagram, and especially your main Gmail. It’s the absolute best way to stop someone from hacking your accounts.

6. Ignore sketchy WhatsApp job offers: If a random international number messages you offering ₹2000 a day just to "like YouTube videos" or "review Google Maps," block them. It’s a classic task-fraud scam designed to drain your bank account.

7. Do your software updates: I know they're annoying and take up storage, but update your phone and laptop! Those updates patch up security holes that hackers love to exploit.

8. Avoid doing banking on free Wi-Fi: Don't pay your semester fees or log into your bank account while connected to the free, unsecured Wi-Fi at a local cafe or the campus library.
