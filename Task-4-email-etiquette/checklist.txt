Social Media Do's and Don'ts for College Students
The Do's:

1. Do Google yourself occasionally: See what potential employers or professors see when they search your name. Clean up anything you wouldn't want them to find.

2. Do build a professional presence: Use platforms like LinkedIn to highlight your projects, skills, and campus involvement. It actually helps a lot when job hunting.

3. Do the "Grandma Test" before posting: If you wouldn't want your grandma or your future boss to see a photo or comment, just don't post it.

4. Do ask before tagging: Respect your friends' privacy. Not everyone wants their location or weekend photos blasted to the public.

5. Do fact-check before hitting share: Don't be the person spreading fake news. Check if a link is actually legit before retweeting it.

The Don'ts:

1. Don't trust privacy settings completely: Platforms update their rules all the time, and people take screenshots. Assume anything you post could eventually go public.

2. Don't vent about professors, classmates, or jobs: Complaining online leaves a permanent, searchable record. It's an easy way to burn bridges before you even graduate.

3. Don't feed the trolls: Getting into heated online arguments or leaving toxic comments isn't worth the stress, and it damages your reputation.

4. Don't post anything that breaks school rules: Avoid posting visual proof of anything that violates your university's code of conduct.

5. Don't overshare: Keep your student ID number, live location, and highly personal drama off your feed to protect your privacy and safety.
