A classic example of poor digital communication is the dreaded, vague text message in a group project chat. Imagine a student quickly typing, "why aren't the slides done?" while rushing to class. To the sender, it’s just a quick question about the project's status. But to the teammate receiving it, it reads like an aggressive, unfair accusation. This kind of tone-deaf messaging often leads to instant defensiveness, a derailed group chat, and a lot of unnecessary stress. This whole mess could easily be avoided by taking an extra five seconds to add context and a polite tone. A much better message would be: "Hey team, I noticed the slides aren't updated yet—is anyone having tech issues, or do we just need more time?" Tone is everything in text, and a little politeness prevents major conflicts.
